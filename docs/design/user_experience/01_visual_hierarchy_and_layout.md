# 01 - Visual Hierarchy and Layout

A good layout reduces cognitive load and guides the eye to the most important point.

## 1. Whitespace

- Negative space is not wasted space; it is the interface’s breathing room.
- Do not pack every piece of information together. Use the shared grid, for example `p-4`, `p-6`, and `gap-4`.
- The more important an element is, the more intentional space it may need around it.

## 2. Scanning Patterns

Users scan rather than read every character:

- **F-pattern:** text-heavy pages are often scanned across the top and then down the left side. Put important content early and toward the left where appropriate.
- **Z-pattern:** landing pages are commonly scanned from left to right, diagonally down, and across again. Place a primary call to action where the visual path naturally ends.

## 3. Typography Contrast

Do not make every piece of text the same size. Use weight, size, color, and opacity to create hierarchy. For example, a heading may use `text-xl font-bold`, while supporting detail uses `text-sm` with a readable muted token.

## 4. Proximity

Related things belong together. The distance between a title and its content should be smaller than the distance separating one information group from another.
